About My Data

For this project, I collected personal golf data between September and November 2025 to explore how environmental conditions and personal factors affect performance and happiness. Each entry represents one full round of golf, recorded with variables including date, weather, temperature, start time, duration, score, and happiness rating after the round.

Sketch 1 – Weather vs Happiness (Scatterplot / Bubble Plot)

Hypothesis: As weather improves (from rainy to sunny), my happiness after playing increases. Longer rounds may also relate to higher happiness, shown by larger bubble sizes.

Data Elements:

  • X-axis: Weather condition (Rainy → Sunny)
  • Y-axis: Happiness After Playing (1–10)
  • Bubble Size: Duration (minutes)
  • Color: Weather Type (blue → yellow gradient)

Units & Ranges:

  • Weather Index: 1–4
  • Happiness After: 4–10 points
  • Duration: 220–310 minutes

Representation:
Each bubble represents one golf round. The horizontal axis shows weather conditions, the vertical axis shows happiness after playing, and bubble size represents duration. Color encodes weather types from blue (rainy) to yellow (sunny). The visualization reveals that sunnier days generally correspond with higher happiness and longer rounds.

Sketch 2 – Temperature vs Score by Exact Start Time (Bar Chart)

Research Question: Does the temperature and exact start time of each round influence my golf score?

Data Elements:

  • X-axis: Exact Start Time (10:30–15:45)
  • Y-axis: Score (strokes)
  • Color: Temperature (°C, blue → yellow gradient)

Units & Ranges:

  • Start Time: 10:30–15:45
  • Score: 87–95 strokes
  • Temperature: 12–18 °C

Representation:
This bar chart visualizes how temperature and start time relate to my golf scores. Each bar represents one recorded round between September and November 2025. The horizontal axis displays the exact start time (10:30 a.m.–3:45 p.m.), while the vertical axis shows the score. Bar color encodes temperature (blue for cooler, yellow for warmer), and all bars have equal width for clarity. The visualization reveals that rounds played at warmer midday times often resulted in slightly lower scores, while early or later rounds under cooler temperatures tended to yield higher scores.

Sketch 3 – Happiness, Duration, and Score (Interactive Visualization)

Research Question: How does my happiness after playing golf relate to my score and the duration of the round?

Data Elements:

  • X-axis: Happiness After Playing (1–10)
  • Y-axis: Score (strokes)
  • Color: Duration (minutes, blue → yellow gradient)
  • Hover Tooltip: Course + Weather + Duration

Units & Ranges:

  • Happiness After: 4–10
  • Score: 87–95 strokes
  • Duration: 220–310 minutes

Representation:
This interactive scatterplot displays happiness on the X-axis and score on the Y-axis. Each circle represents one golf round, with color showing duration (blue for shorter, yellow for longer). Hover tooltips display the course, weather, and duration details. The layout highlights whether higher happiness aligns with better performance and longer rounds.

Summary

These three proposals explore how weather conditions, temperature, and personal experience interact with golf performance and happiness. Sketch 1 examines the relationship between weather and happiness after playing, Sketch 2 focuses on how temperature and exact start time influence golf scores, and Sketch 3 combines happiness, duration, and performance into an interactive visualization to reveal overall patterns. Each design emphasizes clarity, proportional encoding, and visual consistency while applying only techniques introduced in class.
