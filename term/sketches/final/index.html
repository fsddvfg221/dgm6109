About My Data

For this project, I collected personal golf data between September and November 2025 to explore how environmental conditions and personal factors affect performance and happiness. Each entry represents one full round of golf, recorded with variables including date, weather, temperature, start time, duration, score, and happiness rating after the round.

Sketch 1 – Weather vs Happiness (Scatterplot / Bubble Plot)

Research Question: How does weather condition (rainy, cloudy, sunny) influence my happiness after playing golf?

Hypothesis: As weather improves from rainy to sunny, my happiness after playing increases. Additionally, longer rounds may contribute to higher happiness, since extended playtime often occurs during better weather conditions.

Data Elements:

  • X-axis: Weather condition (Rainy → Sunny)
  • Y-axis: Happiness After Playing (1–10)
  • Bubble Size: Duration (minutes)
  • Color: Weather Type (blue → yellow gradient)

Units & Ranges:

  • Weather Index: 1–4
  • Happiness After: 4–10 points
  • Duration: 220–310 minutes

Representation:
Each bubble represents one golf round. The horizontal axis shows weather conditions, the vertical axis shows happiness after playing, and bubble size represents duration. Color encodes weather types from blue (rainy) to yellow (sunny). The visualization reveals that sunnier days generally correspond with higher happiness and longer rounds.

Sketch 2 – Happiness Rings: Weather, Score, and Happiness (Radial Visualization)

Research Question: How do score and weather type distribute across different happiness levels (7–10)?

Hypothesis: As my happiness level increases from 7 to 10, my golf score tends to improve (lower score). Furthermore, sunny weather is expected to appear more frequently in the higher-happiness rings, indicating that better weather contributes to both improved performance and higher emotional satisfaction.

Data Elements:

  • Radial Position: Happiness Level (7–10)
  • Label: Score (87–95 strokes)
  • Color: Weather Type (Sunny, Cloudy, Rainy)

Units & Ranges:

  • Happiness Level: 7–10
  • Score: 87–95 strokes
  • Weather: 3 categories (Sunny / Cloudy / Rainy)

Representation:
This radial visualization uses concentric rings to represent happiness levels from 7 to 10. Each circle represents one golf round. The number inside each circle indicates the score, and the circle’s color shows the weather type: yellow for sunny, purple-gray for cloudy, and blue for rainy. Rounds with higher happiness appear farther outward on the diagram. This layout makes it easy to compare how score and weather conditions distribute across different happiness bands, highlighting whether good weather and better performance tend to align with higher post-round happiness.

Sketch 3 – Radial Weather & Happiness Change Visualization

Research Question: How does weather condition relate to the difference between happiness before and after each round?

Hypothesis: As the difference between happiness after and happiness before increases, emotional improvement becomes more pronounced. In addition, sunny weather is likely associated with larger positive happiness changes, while cloudy or rainy conditions may lead to smaller or negative shifts.

Data Elements:

  • Line Segment Length: Happiness Before vs Happiness After (difference shown by red and green segments)
  • Dot Color: Weather Type (Sunny, Cloudy, Rainy)
  • Radial Angle: Individual Golf Rounds (categorical; one angle per round)

Units & Ranges:

  • Happiness Before: 4–9 (1–10 scale)
  • Happiness After: 7–10 (1–10 scale)
  • Weather Types: Sunny (orange), Cloudy (gray), Rainy (blue)
  • Rounds Represented: 13 total rounds, each placed at a unique radial angle

Representation:
This radial visualization uses outward spokes surrounding a central golf-ball icon to show changes in happiness before and after each round. Each spoke represents a single golf round. The inner red portion of each line shows the reported happiness before playing, while the outer green portion shows the happiness after playing. The difference between the two lengths represents the emotional change during the round. The colored circle at each spoke’s endpoint marks the weather condition: orange for sunny, gray for cloudy, and blue for rainy. This layout allows readers to easily compare: the size of happiness increases across rounds, which weather types are associated with larger positive shifts, and whether some conditions lead to smaller or negative changes.

Summary

These three visualization proposals explore how weather conditions and emotional changes relate to my golf experience, performance, and overall happiness. Each sketch highlights different dimensions of my dataset while following the clarity and proportional-encoding principles emphasized in class.

Sketch 1 uses a weather-to-happiness scatterplot to examine whether sunnier conditions correspond to higher post-round happiness. Sketch 2 introduces a radial “happiness rings” layout that compares weather, score, and happiness levels across all rounds, revealing how performance and mood distribute across different conditions. Sketch 3 expands this perspective by visualizing the change between happiness before and after each round using a spoke-based radial diagram, allowing clear comparison of emotional shifts under sunny, cloudy, or rainy weather.

Together, these proposals present complementary ways of understanding how environmental factors and personal experience influence my golf performance and emotional outcomes. Each sketch remains visually consistent, readable, and grounded in the visualization techniques introduced in class.
