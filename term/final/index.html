Radial Weather & Happiness Change

Research Question

How does weather condition relate to the difference between my happiness before and after each golf round?

Hypothesis

As the difference between happiness after and happiness before increases, emotional improvement becomes more pronounced. In addition, sunny weather is likely associated with larger positive happiness changes, while cloudy or rainy conditions may lead to smaller or more moderate shifts.

Data Collected

This term project tracks my personal golf rounds from September 14 to December 7, 2025. For each round I recorded: date, start time, duration, course, weather, number of holes, score, whether I walked or used a cart, and my self-rated happiness before and after the round on a 1–10 scale.

For this draft visualization, the JSON file includes one record per round with the following properties: date, course, weather, happinessBefore, and happinessAfter. The visualization focuses on the relationship between happiness change and weather type, while the other recorded fields are kept for possible later drafts.

How the Visualization Works

Each spoke in the radial diagram represents one golf round. The spokes radiate out from a central golf-ball icon. The inner red segment shows my happiness before playing, and the outer green segment shows my happiness after playing. The distance from the center is on the same 1–10 happiness scale for all rounds. The colored circle at the end of each spoke shows the weather: orange for sunny or partly sunny days, gray for cloudy or overcast days, and blue for rainy or drizzly days. A legend on the right explains the colors and indicates that distance from the center represents “Happiness (1–10 scale)”.

Patterns and Initial Conclusions

Overall, almost every round shows a positive happiness change. Most spokes have a green segment that is 2–3 units longer than the red base, which means my mood usually improves by about 2–3 points after golf. There are a few extreme days with very large jumps (+4 to +6), and these tend to happen on sunny or lightly rainy days. Cloudy rounds still improve my mood, but their spokes are usually shorter, with increases of only +1 or +2.

Based on this draft, my hypothesis is partly supported: larger happiness changes often appear when the weather is sunny or lightly rainy, while cloudy and overcast days are more likely to produce modest improvements. At the same time, the radial layout also makes it clear that weather is not the only factor: even on rainy days, there are several rounds where my happiness jumps by +3 or more, suggesting that playing golf itself is a strong positive influence regardless of conditions.

Code Notes

The visualization is built with D3 v7 using techniques from class: an SVG canvas, simple configuration variables, Array.map to process the JSON data, a custom classification function for weather types, and basic trigonometry (sine and cosine) to convert radial angles into x/y positions. No external frameworks are used.
